      Split 7.2x fronthaul on the RFSoC DFE

      An engineering reference to eCPRI and the O-RAN CUS-plane, read from the O-RU side: every header down to the bit, how the timing windows and sync plane work, and how the whole thing maps onto a Zynq UltraScale+ RFSoC DFE (ZU6xDR) radio.

      Ethernet / VLAN eCPRI transport O-RAN application headers IQ payload Timing and clocks

      One downlink U-plane frame carrying 53 PRBs of 9-bit BFP IQ in a 1500-byte MTU, drawn to scale. Tap a segment to open its bit layout in section 4. These colors mean the same layer in every diagram below.

      1Where Split 7.2x sits

      The O-RAN lower-layer split cuts the PHY in two. Everything above resource-element mapping stays in the O-DU; the FFT, cyclic prefix and everything toward the antenna live in the O-RU.

      The "x" in 7.2x reflects the two O-RU categories O-RAN allows. A Category A O-RU receives already-precoded layer data, so each spatial stream on the fronthaul maps to a transmit path. A Category B O-RU does precoding itself, which is what enables modulation compression on the downlink and keeps massive-MIMO fronthaul proportional to layers, not antennas. The uplink is asymmetric in both categories: the O-RU does FFT and optional digital beamforming, but channel estimation and equalisation stay in the O-DU.

      4Packet anatomy, bit by bit

      All fields are big-endian (network byte order); bit 0 in the header row is the most significant bit of each byte, matching the O-RAN and eCPRI figures. Hover or tap a field for its meaning.

      Select a field.

      A U-plane packet is Ethernet header + VLAN + eCPRI common header (4 bytes) + eCPRI transport header (4 bytes: eAxC ID and sequence ID) + O-RAN common header (4 bytes) + one or more section headers, each followed by its PRB payload. A C-plane type 1 message has an 8-byte O-RAN common header followed by 8-byte section descriptors, plus any section extensions.

      Two fields that trip everyone. numPrbu and numPrbc are 8 bits, so they cannot say 273. The value 0 means "all PRBs of the carrier". And the U-plane udCompHdr is only present when compression is configured as dynamic over the M-plane. If the O-DU and O-RU disagree on static versus dynamic, the whole PRB payload shifts by two bytes and the IQ turns to noise.

      eAxC ID: the 16-bit stream address

      The eCPRI ecpriRtcid / ecpriPcid field is reinterpreted by O-RAN as the extended Antenna-Carrier ID. Its four sub-fields have widths set per deployment over the M-plane; the only rule is that they sum to 16 and both ends agree. Adjust the split below to see the address space.

      Sequence ID

      ecpriSeqid is an 8-bit Sequence ID incremented per eAxC per plane, then an E-bit and a 7-bit Subsequence ID. O-RAN does not fragment at the eCPRI layer, so in practice E = 1 and Subsequence ID = 0 on every packet. Large symbols are split at the application layer instead, by sending several sections (each covering a PRB range) in separate packets. The O-RU uses the Sequence ID to count lost and out-of-order packets per stream.

      7IQ data formats and compression

      Each PRB is 12 resource elements, each a complex (I, Q) pair. Compression is defined per PRB, selected by udCompMeth and udIqWidth (0 in that field means 16).

      udCompMethMethodPer-PRB parameter (udCompParam)Typical widthBytes per PRB
      0No compressionnone1648
      1Block floating point1 B: 4 reserved + 4-bit exponent9 / 12 / 1428 / 37 / 43
      2Block scaling1 B blockScaler9–1228–37
      3μ-law1 B: compBitWidth + compShift8–925–28
      4Modulation compressionnone (parameters in C-plane ext 4/5)1–4 (QPSK–256QAM)3–12
      5 / 6BFP / mod-comp with selective RE sendingas above + RE mask—varies

      Bytes per PRB is always an integer because 12 × 2 × w bits = 3w bytes. That is why the RFSoC-side decompressor can work on byte-aligned PRB records even for odd widths like 9.

      Block floating point, live

      BFP finds the largest |I| or |Q| in the PRB, picks one shared exponent so the largest value fits in a signed w-bit mantissa, and shifts all 24 values right by it. Drag the width to see the quantisation cost on a random 16-bit OFDM-like PRB.

      10Delay management: the timing windows

      O-RAN measures everything relative to the air interface at the O-RU antenna. The O-RU publishes a delay profile over the M-plane; the O-DU and the transport network must fit around it.

      ParameterMeaningOwner
      T2a_min_up, T2a_max_upWindow, before air time, in which a DL U-plane packet must arrive at the O-RUO-RU (delay profile)
      T2a_min_cp_dl, T2a_max_cp_dlSame, for DL C-planeO-RU

      T12_min, T12_max, T34_min, T34_maxTransport delay DL and UL, including fibre and switchesNetwork
      T1a_min/max, Ta4_min/maxO-DU transmit window (DL) and receive window (UL)O-DU (derived)

      Defaults are illustrative, not spec values. Fibre is taken as 5 µs/km; T12_min = fibre, T12_max = fibre + switch delay variation; T34 is assumed equal to T12.

      The two inequalities behind the picture are the whole of delay management. Downlink: the O-DU must send at T1a such that T2a_min ≤ T1a − T12 ≤ T2a_max for every transport delay, which gives T1a_min = T2a_min + T12_max and T1a_max = T2a_max + T12_min. This only has a solution if the O-RU's receive window is wider than the transport's delay variation. Uplink: Ta4_min = Ta3_min + T34_min and Ta4_max = Ta3_max + T34_max.

      What this means for the RFSoC design. T2a_max_up − T2a_min_up is set by how much DL symbol buffering you build. A wider window tolerates more network jitter but costs BRAM or URAM, since you must store every early symbol for every eAxC until its air time. T2a_min_up is your worst-case O-RU processing latency plus margin: iFFT, CP insertion, channel filter, CFR, DPD and the RF-DAC datapath (section 14).

      An illustrative ±1.5 µs budget

      Illustrative allocation in the spirit of ITU-T G.8271.1; real budgets depend on hop count, boundary-clock class (G.8273.2 Class B: cTE ≤ 20 ns per hop, Class C: ≤ 10 ns) and link asymmetry.

      Deriving the radio frame from PTP time

      O-RAN ties the radio frame to the GPS epoch (6 Jan 1980). The O-RU computes frameId = ⌊(t_GPS − offset) / 10 ms⌋ mod 256, where the offset is built from two M-plane parameters: α (a fine offset in units of 1/1.2288 ns) and β (a whole number of 10 ms frames). The 10 ms boundary becomes a hardware pulse that re-aligns the O-RAN interface timers, the Low-PHY symbol counter and the DUC/DDC NCO phase reset.

      PTP runs with the G.8275.1 telecom profile for full timing support: Layer 2 multicast (EtherType 0x88F7, non-forwardable address 01-80-C2-00-00-0E), 16 Sync and 16 Delay_Req per second, with SyncE providing the frequency. Hardware timestamps must be taken at the PCS so that the RS-FEC and gearbox latencies are either excluded or calibrated out.

      Frequency planning checks

      Direct RF sampling puts harmonics, interleaving spurs and NCO images in places a superheterodyne radio never sees. For each band, check the RF-DAC image at fs − f_RF, the RF-ADC Nyquist zone and its alias of the Tx band in FDD, and interleaving spurs at fs/2 ± f and fs/4 ± f against the receive band. Chapter 12 and Appendix F of the RFSoC book walk through this with worked frequency plans; keep every converter rate an integer multiple of 122.88 MHz so the frame timing stays exact.

      Illustrative order-of-magnitude values for a 100 MHz, 30 kHz carrier; measure on your build (ILA timestamps against the ToD counter). The FFT stages cost about one symbol each because a whole symbol must be collected before the transform can finish.

      Buffer sizing. DL symbol buffer per eAxC ≈ window depth in symbols × PRBs × 48 B (decompressed 16-bit). With a 300 µs window at 30 kHz (≈ 9 symbols), 273 PRBs and 8 eAxC that is about 9 × 273 × 48 × 8 ≈ 0.94 MB, more than the ZU67DR's block RAM alone would comfortably hold alongside everything else, so store compressed and decompress on read, or use URAM.
